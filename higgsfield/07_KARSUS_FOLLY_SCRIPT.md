**Runtime:** about 100 s. **Format:** 16:9, 1080p.
**Style:** photoreal game cinematic. Magic is shown as **long, continuous events** (critique of *The Remembering* v1, 2026-10-30).
**Hero:** Karsus, the greatest archwizard of Netheril, played by Blagoj Nikolov from **`Blagoj-Base`** (`f5e2e917…`). He is human, so the race layer is empty and likeness is as high as it gets.

## 1. Lore spine (canon; everything else is original)
- **Netheril** was a human empire of archwizards who lived in **flying cities ("enclaves") carved from inverted mountains**, kept aloft by magical engines called *mythallars*.
- The **phaerimm**, monsters under the sand, were draining the life and magic of the land and turning it into desert (the future Anauroch).
- To stop them, **Karsus** wrote the only spell of its kind ever made, *Karsus's Avatar*. It was meant to take the divine power of **Mystryl**, goddess of magic, for himself.
- In **−339 DR** he cast it, and for an instant he was a god. He could not hold the whole Weave of magic. To save magic itself, **Mystryl died**.
  - All magic failed at once and **the flying cities fell from the sky**.
  - Mystryl was reborn as Mystra, who changed the rules so that such a spell could never be cast again.
- Karsus was **turned to stone**. His petrified figure lies in the High Forest, and the town of Karse later grew around it.

**Theme:** a man who tried to save his people by **taking** what can only be **given**. It's a tragedy of pride with a noble motive, not a story of good against evil.

## 4. The narrator's voice: what the research says, and what I chose

**Who speaks.** An **old Karsus, remembering from inside the stone.** It is the same man, so the audience hears the tragedy from the one who caused it, and it ties the final image of the stone face to the voice. Alternative: a neutral chronicler (a bard reading a history). I recommend the old Karsus, but this is your call.

**What the evidence supports** (details and caveats in the references):
1. **Slow and quiet reads as sorrow.** Reviews of vocal emotion report that sad speech has a **slow speech rate, low intensity, little variation in loudness, low high-frequency energy and a flatter pitch contour** (Juslin & Laukka 2003, as summarised by secondary sources; I could not open the original, so verify before quoting it).
2. **A lower pitch reads as authority.** The link between low pitch and perceived dominance is robust across studies; the link to trustworthiness is weaker and depends on context (Klofstad et al. 2012; later reviews).
3. **Restraint is the trailer-narrator convention.** Practitioner guides say the epic narrator does not shout, does not whisper, keeps a controlled level, and slows down for reveals. These are **vendor and freelancer sources, so they are craft folklore, not evidence.**

**Caveat I want you to see.** The sadness-and-low-pitch finding is not universal: in English and Hindi, sad speech was **higher** in pitch than neutral speech in one study, while in German and Arabic it was lower. So I am **not** asking for the lowest possible voice. The weight comes from slowness, softness and body, not from rumbling bass. All the pitch studies are small lab experiments, so treat them as guidance.

**Voice specification (for ElevenLabs Voice Design):**

| Property | Setting | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Age | late 60s to 70s | He has stood in stone for centuries; age lends weight and sorrow |
| Gender | male | matches the character |
| Pitch | **low-mid baritone, not bass**, with a slight roughness | Authority without a cliché trailer growl; see the caveat |
| Pace | **slow, about 100-115 words per minute**, with a pause after every sentence | Sadness and gravitas; the film needs the silence between lines |
| Loudness | **soft and even**, never raised, never a whisper | Restraint; he is remembering, not performing |
| Timbre ("colour") | **dark and warm, like dry amber**: body in the chest, low brightness, a little breath at line ends | Low high-frequency energy reads as sorrow; warmth keeps him human |
| Accent | cultured neutral English (light received pronunciation) | A scholar-king, an archwizard of an old empire |
| Emotion | **grave regret under control**: no sobbing, no trembling, a slight lift in "He only wished to save them" | Pride and grief; the restraint is what hurts |
| Avoid | gravelly "movie-trailer guy", shouting, whispering, any vocal smile | They break the tone |

The design prompt will use these words. ElevenLabs gives 3 previews and **you pick**, as before. I will compare the 3 with `tools/voice_stats.py` (pitch, pace, brightness) against this table, but your ear decides.

## 5. Music and sound
- SP1-SP3: slow build with low strings and a wordless choir, rising with the rings.
- SP4: the choir holds a single chord. **No voice.**
- **SP5: hard cut to absolute silence when magic dies.** This is the film's most powerful moment, and it only works because the narrator has also stopped. Then only deep rumble and wind; low brass returns as the cities fall.
- SP6: solo instrument and forest ambience, under N4.
- Mix: the narrator sits about 6 dB above the score, with the score ducked under each line (as in *The Remembering*).

## 9. Risks (the mentor part)
1. **A 22 s continuous magic event is the hardest thing to make in AI video.** Two chained 11 s clips can show a seam where they join. Mitigation: the end frame of clip A is literally the start frame of clip B, and I check every join frame by frame. If a join shows, I hide it with a 2 s insert.
2. **"Every light goes out" is hard to prompt.** Models like to *add* light, not remove it. Mitigation: two keyframes (lit and dark) as start and end, so the model is forced to interpolate the blackout.
3. **Mystryl as a woman made of light** is my artistic choice. Canon gives no single physical form. It's kept abstract to avoid a "generic goddess" look.
4. **Your likeness** appears only in the inserts and two push-ins. If those fail the authenticity check, they're re-made from the `Blagoj-Base` views before any video is generated.
