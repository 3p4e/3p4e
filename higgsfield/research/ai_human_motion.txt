6 Best AI Video Tools for Realistic Human Motion in 2026 (Tested and Compared)
Higgsfield
10 min
For realistic human motion in 2026, Kling 4.0 and Higgsfield Cinema Studio 4.0 performed best in our test: both gave a usable take every time when walking, sitting and handling a glass. Kling 4.0 costs least per usable clip ($1.08), Cinema Studio 4.0 keeps one character across shots, and HeyGen or Synthesia fit talking presenters.
Most AI video tools can put a person in a frame. The hard part is making that person move like a real body: feet that stay planted, weight that shifts from leg to leg, fingers that close around an object, and a face and outfit that stay the same. We gave the same three tasks to four models: walk and turn, sit down and stand up, pick up a glass and put it back. Copying motion from a video was tested separately.
Looking for prompting techniques rather than a tool comparison? See
How to Make AI People Move Realistically.
Why Human Motion Is the Hardest Problem in AI Video
Getting a convincing environment right is mostly a texture problem. Getting an object right is mostly a geometry problem. Getting a person to move convincingly is all three problems at once, plus physics, plus performance, plus frame-to-frame consistency. The weight has to load before the action and release after it, and all of that has to hold across every frame without the model defaulting to an average.

Quick Pick by Task
Walking and turning: Kling 4.0 or Higgsfield Cinema Studio 4.0, every take usable with a locked camera.
Sitting down and standing up: any of the four tested models; Kling 4.0 costs least per usable clip.
Hands and objects: Kling 4.0, Cinema Studio 4.0 or Seedance 2.5, every take usable.
Copying motion from a video: Kling Motion Control 3.0.
One character across several shots: Cinema Studio 4.0.
A talking presenter: HeyGen or Synthesia.
How These Tools Compare
Usable takes out of 3 per task, out of 9 in total. All four models ran on Higgsfield: image-to-video, 8 seconds, 720p, same start frame and prompt for every model. Runway and Luma were not part of this test.

Higgsfield Cinema Studio 4.0: One Character Across a Sequence
Higgsfield is a generative platform for creative production, with 35+ frontier models for generating images and turning them into video scenes. Cinema Studio 4.0, released in August 2026, is its directing tool, with a built-in model of the same name. Instead of relying only on a prompt, you set genre, camera, lens, lighting, palette, tempo and emotion before generation.
Genres: General, Action, Epic, Drama, Comedy, Horror and Noir.
Camera: 30+ movement presets, four camera bodies, five lens types, apertures from f/1.4 to f/11.
Look: 50+ color palettes and a lighting console with six presets.
Continuity: up to 50 references, Forward and Backward Extend, up to 30 seconds per generation.
Kling 4.0, Veo 3.1 and Seedance 2.5 can be picked inside Cinema Studio 4.0 on the same credits, but the genre, camera, palette and lighting controls apply only to Cinema Studio 4.0’s own models.
What we tested:
image-to-video, 8 s, 720p, audio off; then a three-scene sequence: reference → generation → two Extends.
Where it held:
locked camera in every take, the left hand on the glass as asked, and the same face and outfit across all three scenes.
Where it broke:
the first frame is re-rendered about 4% darker; one sequence take cut to a close-up despite Single shot pacing.
Who it is for:
a scene built from several shots of the same person.
Pricing:
28 credits ($1.37) per 4 seconds at 720p. Plus $49/mo (1,000 credits), Ultra $129/mo (3,000 credits)

Kling 4.0: Complex Human Movement and Video Editing
Kling 4.0 is designed for complex human movement, including fast action, gestures and character interactions. Available to everyone since October 15, 2026, it adds 3- to 30-second clips, up to 10 keyframes and up to 15 references. It also edits existing video through Creative Recreation and targeted edits.
What we tested:
Kling 4.0 on Higgsfield, image-to-video, 8 s, 720p. Sound is added by default.
Where it held:
every take started on the start frame, the camera stayed locked, and the fingers closed around the glass and released it.
Where it broke:
sound cannot be switched off on Higgsfield.
Who it is for:
body movement and hand contact that must look right on the first try, at the lowest cost per usable clip.
Pricing:
22 credits ($1.08) per 8-second 720p clip on Higgsfield; launch discounts can lower this.

Seedance 2.5: Clean Contact, Loose With the Start Frame
Seedance 2.5 from ByteDance runs on Higgsfield and in Dreamina, ByteDance’s own platform.
What we tested:
Seedance 2.5 on Higgsfield, omni-reference with the image as start frame, 8 s, 720p, audio off.
Where it held:
locked camera, clean sitting and standing, the left hand on the glass as asked.
Where it broke:
one walking take started from a different framing, with the character smaller and further back; the first frame is re-rendered slightly darker.
Who it is for:
full-body movement and object handling when the opening frame need not match exactly.
Pricing:
56 credits ($2.74) per 8 seconds on Higgsfield; in Dreamina, Basic $15/mo for 1,575 credits.

Veo 3.1: Full Walks, Wrong Hand
Veo 3.1 from Google DeepMind is Google’s current video generation model. It combines detailed environments, native audio and prompt-based control, useful for scenes where both setting and character matter. It takes reference images for character appearance, style references, first and last frames and extension.
What we tested:
Veo 3.1 (quality) on Higgsfield, image-to-video, 8 s, 720p, prompt enhancement and sound on by default.
Where it held:
every take started on the start frame and completed the walk and the sit-stand.
Where it broke:
in all three glass takes it used the right hand instead of the left and lifted the glass to chest height; the camera drifted slightly in every walk. Identity still needs checking across generations.
Who it is for:
scenes where environment and sound matter as much as the person.
Pricing:
80 credits ($3.92) per 8 seconds on Higgsfield. Google AI Plus $4.99/mo (200 Flow credits), Pro $19.99/mo (1,000), Ultra from $99.99/mo (10,000).

Runway Gen-4.5: Prompt-Led Control
Gen-4.5 handles text- and image-to-video generation, while Act-Two transfers recorded performances to a character. Runway also provides separate tools for editing footage, such as Aleph. Camera angle, body position and movement can be written directly into the prompt. Runway Ads, launched in September 2026, is a separate workflow that generates ad variants and publishes them to Meta, Google and TikTok.
Runway was not part of our test.
Pricing:
Gen-4.5 uses 12 credits per second. Standard $15/mo (625 credits), Pro $35/mo (2,250), Max $95/mo (9,500).
Luma Ray3.2: Image-to-Video in Dream Machine
Ray3.2 is Luma’s current video model in Dream Machine, and Luma Modify re-renders a recorded performance with a new character or look. Luma was not part of our test.
Pricing:
100 credits per 5 seconds and 300 per 10 seconds at 720p. Plus $30/mo (10,000 credits), Pro $90/mo (40,000), Ultra $300/mo (150,000).

What If You Need a Talking Presenter?
For a presenter who talks to the camera, avatar platforms fit better than video models. They were not part of the motion test.
HeyGen Avatar V:
presenter-led videos with natural gestures, facial expressions and lip-synced speech in 175+ languages; motion prompts direct gestures and movement. 48 credits per minute; Pro from $49/mo for 1,000 credits, about $2.35 per minute. Creator $29/mo (600 credits).
Synthesia:
designed for scripted communication, training and presenter videos rather than complex action; supports different backgrounds and generated B-roll. Starter $19/mo (1,250 credits, about 12 minutes, ~$1.58 per minute), Pro $89/mo; since October 2026 credits are shared across video and other features.
Which Tool Should You Use?
If you want the lowest cost per usable clip, choose Kling 4.0: it was usable in every task. Higgsfield Cinema Studio 4.0 was also usable in every task and fits best when one character has to carry a sequence of shots. Seedance 2.5 handled sitting and the glass cleanly but was looser with the start frame, while Veo 3.1 completed full walks with environment and sound but used the wrong hand on the glass.
To copy movement from a video onto a new character, use Kling Motion Control 3.0, keeping in mind that its clips are short. Runway Gen-4.5 with Act-Two suits prompt-led control and performance capture, and Luma Ray3.2 with Modify suits generation and re-rendering of recorded performances. Neither Runway nor Luma was part of our test.

Got any questions left?
Can I copy movement from a real video?
Yes. Kling Motion Control 3.0 transfers a reference video of at least 3 seconds onto a character image. Runway Act-Two maps a driving video onto a character, and Luma Modify re-renders a recorded performance with a new character.
How do I keep the same character across clips?
Use the same reference image and describe the outfit the same way each time. In our test, Cinema Studio 4.0 held the face and outfit across three scenes with Extend. Kling 4.0 takes up to 15 references.
What is the difference between Higgsfield Cinema Studio 4.0 and Kling 4.0?
Kling 4.0 is a generation model. Cinema Studio 4.0 is a directing tool with its own model: you set genre, camera, lens, lighting, tempo and emotion before generation, then add references and Extend.
Can I use Kling 4.0 without a separate subscription?
Yes. Kling 4.0 runs on Higgsfield on the same credit balance as Veo 3.1, Seedance 2.5 and Cinema Studio 4.0, so you don’t need a separate Kling plan.
What is the difference between an avatar platform and a generation model?
Avatar platforms such as HeyGen and Synthesia make presenter videos with lip sync and translation. Generation models such as Kling 4.0, Seedance 2.5, Veo 3.1 and Runway Gen-4.5 handle full-body motion and the environment.
What does emotion control do for motion quality?
It sets the performance arc before generation. Picking an emotion such as Fear or Joy builds it into the body movement from the first frame, so the model doesn’t default to a neutral performance.
