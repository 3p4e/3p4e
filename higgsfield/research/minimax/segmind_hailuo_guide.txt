Essential Prompt Structure and Components
To achieve the best video outputs from Hailuo Minimax, understanding the structure of your prompts is key.
A well-crafted prompt can significantly enhance the quality of your video, ensuring it matches your vision while maintaining high consistency and professionalism.
Below are the essential components of a successful Hailuo Minimax prompt:
The Core Hailuo Prompt Formula
A strong prompt follows a specific formula that helps guide the platform in generating the desired video. The key elements of the prompt include:
[Camera Shot + Motion]
: Define the type of shot (close-up, wide shot, etc.) and any dynamic camera movement (e.g., dolly zoom, tracking shots).
[Subject + Description]
: Clearly describe the subject(s) of the video, including appearance, clothing, and other distinguishing features.
[Action]
: Specify what the subject(s) are doing (e.g., running, jumping, interacting with objects).
[Scene + Description]
: Detail the setting or environment where the action takes place (e.g., a busy street, a serene beach).
[Lighting]
: Indicate the type of lighting (e.g., bright, dark, natural, artificial).
[Style/Mood]
: Describe the overall mood or style of the video (e.g., cinematic, dramatic, cheerful).
The order of these elements is crucial. Proper sequencing ensures that the AI understands the relationships between each component, ultimately producing a video that aligns with your creative vision.
Camera Shot and Movement Specifications
To give your video a professional feel, it's important to specify camera shots and movements accurately. Here are some common terms and techniques to use in your prompts:
Basic Shots
: Close-up, medium shot, wide shot, and extreme close-up.
Dynamic Movements
: Add motion to your shots, such as dolly zoom, pan left/right, tracking shots, and drone shots.
Advanced Cinematography
: Include more sophisticated movements like Dutch angles, rack focus, and match cuts.
These movements and shots help create a more engaging visual experience and should be described clearly in your prompts for the best results.
Subject and Character Descriptions
A clear description of the subject or character is essential for consistency throughout your video. When defining a subject, include details such as:
Physical Appearance
: Hair color, skin tone, clothing, and any distinguishing features.
Emotional States
: Descriptions of facial expressions, emotions, and body language that enhance storytelling.
In longer videos or multi-shot sequences, it's important to maintain consistency in how the character looks and acts. This ensures that the subject remains recognizable and the story feels cohesive.

Advanced Prompting Techniques for Cinematic Results
To take your video generation to the next level, applying advanced cinematography techniques and refining your prompts will result in more dynamic, professional-quality videos.
These techniques help elevate the visual storytelling and make your content stand out. Below are several strategies to enhance your prompts:
Creating Dynamic Camera Movements
Camera movements play a significant role in how your video is perceived. When used effectively, they can add excitement, focus attention, or enhance the emotional impact of a scene. Here are some techniques to consider:
Tracking and Following Shots
: Use phrases like “camera tracks behind” or “dolly forward” to create a sense of movement and immersion.
Establishing and Reveal Shots
: “Camera pulls back to reveal” or “zoom out to show” can help establish the scene and introduce new elements.
POV and Perspective Changes
: Describing shots from a first-person view or an over-the-shoulder shot adds a personal touch, drawing the viewer deeper into the story.
These camera movements allow you to add depth and energy to your videos, making them more engaging and cinematic.
Mastering Character Consistency
Maintaining consistent character appearance and behavior throughout a video is essential, especially when working with multiple shots or scenes. To ensure continuity:
Anchor Features
: Define key physical traits or accessories (e.g., a red hat or a distinct necklace) that can help the AI maintain visual consistency.
Multi-Angle Descriptions
: Specify how the character should look from different angles to prevent discrepancies in appearance.
Handling Multiple Characters
: Be clear about each character’s distinct features and actions to avoid confusion or mixing of attributes between characters.
With detailed descriptions, you can ensure that the character stays recognizable and true to the vision throughout the entire sequence.
Environmental and Scene Enhancement
The setting and atmosphere of your video contribute greatly to its overall impact. Adding detailed environmental descriptions will make your scenes more immersive and realistic:
Weather and Lighting
: Specify the weather (e.g., sunny, rainy, foggy) and the lighting conditions (e.g., golden hour, low light, bright overhead) to match the mood of your video.
Time of Day
: The time of day can significantly change the look and feel of your video. Whether it’s morning, afternoon, or night, describing the lighting and shadows will add realism.
Setting Details
: Include specifics like architecture, nature, or even background objects (e.g., "city skyline" or "cozy living room") to ground the viewer in the scene.
Rich environmental details help make your video more visually captivating and believable.

Common Prompting Mistakes and Fixes
Even experienced creators can face challenges when working with AI video generation. Understanding the common mistakes and how to avoid them will help you get the best possible results from Hailuo Minimax.
Below are some of the most frequent prompting issues and practical solutions to improve your video outputs:
H3: Prompt Ambiguity and Vagueness Issues
One of the most common issues in AI video generation is ambiguity in the prompt. If the description is too vague, the AI might not fully understand your vision, resulting in a lackluster or off-target output.
Problem
: Generic descriptions like "a person walking in the park" can lead to bland, uninteresting videos.
Solution
: Be specific with your descriptions. Instead of just “walking,” describe the action in more detail, like “a woman jogging through a park with trees swaying in the breeze, sunlight filtering through the leaves.” This will give the AI more context and direction.
Example
: Compare vague prompts like “a person at a table” to more detailed prompts such as “a man sitting at a wooden table, holding a cup of coffee, with soft sunlight coming through the window.”
Clear, descriptive language helps the AI understand the nuances of your video and generate a more precise output.
Character and Object Consistency Problems
Maintaining consistency with characters and objects across different scenes can be challenging. Small discrepancies, such as changes in a character’s clothing or appearance, can break immersion and confuse the viewer.
Problem
: Inconsistencies in character appearance, such as a character's hair color changing between scenes, or a product looking different from one angle to another.
Solution
: Use
anchor features
— specific descriptors that help maintain consistency. For example, describe the character’s hairstyle, facial features, and outfit clearly in every shot: "Character X wears a red jacket with a blue scarf, with short curly hair."
Solution
: Include details like “same outfit throughout” or “same background” to avoid mismatches in visual continuity.
By reinforcing key descriptors, you ensure that characters and objects remain visually consistent, especially in complex sequences or multi-shot videos.
Technical and Generation Errors
Sometimes, technical issues or errors in the video generation process can lead to incomplete or unsatisfactory results. While these may not always be your fault, understanding potential problems and how to fix them can improve your experience.
Problem
Server errors
like “504” can disrupt video generation.
Solution
: If you encounter a server issue, try submitting your prompt again later or adjusting the complexity of your request to reduce load.
Problem
Style Inconsistency
: The video might start in one visual style but then shift to another.
Solution
: Be consistent with your visual style descriptions. For example, specify “film noir style throughout” or “modern aesthetic” consistently in every prompt.
Proble
m: Camera Movement Failures: The AI may not always interpret complex camera movements accurately.
Solution: Use simpler, clearer instructions for camera movements. For example, instead of “camera tracks slowly to the left,” try “slow tracking shot left, focusing on the subject's face.”
By keeping your instructions clear and consistent, you reduce the chances of these issues arising.
