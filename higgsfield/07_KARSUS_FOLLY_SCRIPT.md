**Runtime:** about 100 s. **Format:** 16:9, 1080p.
**Style:** photoreal game cinematic. Magic is shown as **long, continuous events** (critique of *The Remembering* v1, 2026-10-30).
**Hero:** Karsus, the greatest archwizard of Netheril, played by Blagoj Nikolov from **`Blagoj-Base`** (`f5e2e917…`). He is human, so the race layer is empty and likeness is as high as it gets.

## 1. Lore spine (canon; everything else is original)
- **Netheril** was a human empire of archwizards who lived in **flying cities ("enclaves") carved from inverted mountains**, kept aloft by magical engines called *mythallars*.
- The **phaerimm**, monsters under the sand, were draining the life and magic of the land and turning it into desert (the future Anauroch).
- To stop them, **Karsus** wrote the only spell of its kind ever made, *Karsus's Avatar*. It was meant to take the divine power of **Mystryl**, goddess of magic, for himself.
- In **−339 DR** he cast it, and for an instant he was a god. He could not hold the whole Weave of magic. To save magic itself, **Mystryl died**.
  - All magic failed at once and **the flying cities fell from the sky**.
  - Mystryl was reborn as Mystra, who changed the rules so that such a spell could never be cast again.
- Karsus was **turned to stone**. His petrified figure lies in the High Forest, and the town of Karse later grew around it.

**Theme:** a man who tried to save his people by **taking** what can only be **given**. It's a tragedy of pride with a noble motive, not a story of good against evil.

## 4. Voices: what the research says, and what I chose

Two voices: **Karsus** (also the old echo in the last line) and **Mystryl**. ElevenLabs Voice Design, 3 previews each; you pick.

**What the evidence supports** (caveats below and in the references):
1. **Slow and quiet reads as sorrow:** sad speech has a slow rate, low intensity, little loudness variation, low high-frequency energy and a flatter pitch contour (Juslin & Laukka 2003, from secondary summaries; I could not open the original, so verify details and page numbers).
2. **Lower pitch reads as authority** (Klofstad et al. 2012; Puts et al. 2007). The link to trustworthiness is weaker.
3. **Restraint is the epic-narration convention** (vendor and freelancer sources: craft opinion, not evidence).

**Caveat:** the sad-and-low-pitch finding is not universal. In English and Hindi, sad speech was *higher* in pitch than neutral speech in one study. So the weight comes from slowness, softness and body, not from bass. The pitch studies are small lab experiments: guidance only.

| Property | KARSUS | MYSTRYL |
|---|---|---|
| Age, gender | male, 40s (last line: the same voice aged to a faint old echo) | female, ageless |
| Pitch | low-mid baritone, not bass, a little rough | low-mid alto, not whispery |
| Pace | slow, ~110-120 wpm, a pause after every sentence | slower still, ~90-100 wpm |
| Loudness | controlled and even; one rise at "Only for a moment" | soft and even, never raised |
| Colour (timbre) | **dark amber**: warm, body in the chest, low brightness | **pale silver-warm**: calm and immense, warm rather than cold; layered into a faint chorus in post |
| Accent | cultured neutral English (light received pronunciation): a scholar-king | the same, softer |
| Emotion | proud and tired; strain turning almost tender in SP4 | gentle sorrow, not authority: she is pleading |
| Avoid | gravelly trailer growl, shouting, vocal smile | angelic sweetness, cold detachment |

Compare the 3 previews per voice with `tools/voice_stats.py` (pitch, pace, brightness) against this table; your ear decides.

## 9. Risks (the mentor part)
1. **A 22 s continuous magic event is the hardest thing to make in AI video.** Two chained 11 s clips can show a seam where they join. Mitigation: the end frame of clip A is literally the start frame of clip B, and I check every join frame by frame. If a join shows, I hide it with a 2 s insert.
2. **"Every light goes out" is hard to prompt.** Models like to *add* light, not remove it. Mitigation: two keyframes (lit and dark) as start and end, so the model is forced to interpolate the blackout.
3. **Mystryl as a woman made of light** is my artistic choice. Canon gives no single physical form. It's kept abstract to avoid a "generic goddess" look.
4. **Your likeness** appears only in the inserts and two push-ins. If those fail the authenticity check, they're re-made from the `Blagoj-Base` views before any video is generated.

## 11. Stabilised prompts for Seedance 2.0 Mini (v4, 2026-10-31): these replace every scene description above

**What went wrong in the first Mini test** (`3616a160…`): the golden skiffs and birds I asked for were rendered as helicopters and flying fish; the floating cities appeared to climb out of the clouds. The model invents a motion for every small or vague object and for every verb of change.

**Rules (apply to every prompt from now on):**
1. **One mover.** The camera is the only thing that moves, plus at most one named effect per shot (a spreading wave of sand, a blackout). Everything else is described as already there and motionless.
2. **No small objects.** No skiffs, boats, birds, people, animals or vehicles anywhere. Small things are what the model turns into helicopters and fish. The sky is "empty of other objects".
3. **No verbs of arrival** for fixed things: never "emerge", "rise", "appear", "grow", "float up". Cities "hang motionless"; clouds "stay flat, far below and unchanged".
4. **Positive wording only.** No "no cuts", "no text", "never in close-up": a negated word still primes the model. "Single continuous shot" is enough.
5. **Concrete counts and positions** ("three cities", "from the right edge"), short sentences, one idea each, about 90-120 words.
6. **State changes use start and end frames** (lit to dark, whole to ruined), generated as stills first, not described in prose.
7. **Faces only in the medium-shot and push-in prompts**, with the bust and front costume images as references. Wide shots show only a small robed figure.
